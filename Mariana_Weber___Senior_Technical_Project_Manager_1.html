Background

The Federal Reserve mandated that all U.S. financial institutions adopt ISO 20022 — a sweeping global standard for how payment data is structured and exchanged between banks. For FirstBank, this meant the existing wire infrastructure underpinning Internet Cash Management (ICM) and Treasury Management had to be fully replaced and certified. There was no workaround, no extension. Miss the deadline, face consequences.

The program ran across two distinct phases: the Finastra Fusion Global Pay integration kicking off in October 2020, and the final ISO 20022 messaging compliance work certifying in August 2024 — spanning two cross-functional teams over four years.

What Was Built

The core technical work replaced FirstBank's legacy wire API infrastructure with a new architecture centered on Finastra Fusion Global Pay. The front-end ICM application was rebuilt to consume Profiles stateful authentication and route all wire requests through new PAY endpoints — replacing every primary and secondary wire use case without adding net-new functionality. A clean, certified swap.

The second phase tackled ISO 20022 field-level requirements directly: Java and mobile layers updated to validate required attributes on wire initiation and template updates — including mandatory beneficiary name/address, ABA bank address data sourced from a Mainframe process, and end-to-end outgoing scenario coverage.

  • Replaced all primary and secondary wire use cases via Finastra-compatible PAY endpoints — zero net-new feature scope
  • Integrated Profiles stateful authentication across ICM and Treasury Management wire flows
  • Validated ISO 20022 required fields at application level: beneficiary name/address, ABA bank address, account number bank types
  • Updated Java, mobile, and testing layers to enforce compliance requirements on wire initiation and template updates
  • Coordinated customer communications to ICM customers on new wire template field requirements ahead of go-live
  • Developed migration timeline plan for existing wire templates to reach ISO 20022 compliance
  • Remediated security vulnerabilities and delivered WCAG accessibility improvements across customer-facing wire pages in parallel
The Vendor Challenge

Finastra is a global fintech vendor — not a team you can pull into a retro. The integration depended on Finastra's PAY endpoint specs, Swagger API definitions, and their own release calendar, all of which had to be tracked and negotiated externally while internal teams planned their delivery against them.

Managing this dependency meant translating vendor uncertainty into internal planning buffers, escalating blockers through the right channels, and keeping TM/ICM and Payment Systems teams from building on assumptions that could shift without notice.

Regulatory Pressure

The Federal Reserve's ISO 20022 deadline was non-negotiable. Every sprint decision had to be weighed against certification readiness — there was no room to defer what mattered.

This required radical prioritization, clear dependency tracking across Java, mobile, and testing skill sets, and the discipline to surface risks early — with mitigation options in hand, not just flags raised.

Key Takeaway

A multi-year program with a hard regulatory deadline, a global vendor dependency, two cross-functional teams, and simultaneous security and accessibility mandates. What made it work wasn't that every variable was controlled — it wasn't. It's that risks were surfaced early, trade-offs were made deliberately, and the team always knew what the next most important thing was. ISO 20022 certified. Customers notified. Zero critical incidents at launch.

Background

FirstBank's Account Management systems were built on aging Java infrastructure — functional, but increasingly expensive to maintain, slow to change, and a growing liability as the business needed more agility. The program set out to replace those legacy systems entirely with Appian, a modern low-code platform, without disrupting day-to-day account management operations during the transition.

This wasn't a lift-and-shift. Every process had to be rethought in the context of what Appian makes possible — involving business stakeholders, operations, engineering, and QA across a multi-year delivery arc.

The PM Challenge

Multi-year modernization programs are uniquely difficult to sustain. They outlast individual sprints, outlast quarterly priorities, and often outlast the patience of stakeholders who want to see faster results. Keeping a program like this on track requires more than a Gantt chart — it requires consistent stakeholder alignment, ruthless backlog discipline, and the ability to explain why the current phase matters in terms of the overall transformation, not just what shipped this sprint.

The Appian migration also required ongoing coordination with the vendor's implementation patterns, training internal teams on the platform, and managing the gap between what business stakeholders wanted and what could realistically be built in a low-code environment versus the old custom Java system.

Key Takeaway

Legacy replacement programs succeed when they stay grounded — in the user, in the data, in the actual operational pain being solved. Appian delivered a faster, more maintainable platform. The discipline was in keeping the program honest about scope, sequence, and tradeoffs across a delivery arc that spanned years, not sprints.

Background

FirstBank executed two major bank acquisitions within a compressed timeframe — the CA Bank & Trust (CB&T) acquisition and the ongoing PNC customer transition. Each required migrating thousands of Treasury Management customers onto FirstBank's platform: moving accounts, credentials, entitlements, Salesforce records, and digital banking access while keeping those customers operational throughout.

Mariana owned the digital and Treasury Management workstreams for both conversions — coordinating front-end cutover, Salesforce data migration, system integration, and customer communications across two organizations simultaneously.

CB&T Conversion — Completed

The CA Bank & Trust conversion ran across two tracks, each requiring synchronized cutover of Treasury front-end access, Salesforce customer records, and Digital Banking entitlements. This involved coordinating with a cross-org team spanning FirstBank's Treasury Operations, Digital Banking, Salesforce administration, and the acquired bank's own staff — many of whom were simultaneously onboarding to FirstBank systems and processes.

  • Coordinated Treasury front-end cutover — ICM access, entitlements, and product provisioning for converted accounts
  • Managed Salesforce data migration — customer records, relationship data, and activity history
  • Led high-touch client outreach program — direct contact with 43+ commercial clients ahead of conversion weekend
  • Coordinated Digital Banking user migration — credential transitions and access continuity
  • Zero revenue-impacting incidents on conversion weekend across both tracks
PNC Conversion — Ongoing

The PNC transition is currently active, with Mariana owning the same digital and Treasury Management workstreams. The complexity is larger in scale — more customers, more systems, more stakeholder surface area — and runs concurrently with other active programs in the TM portfolio.

Key Takeaway

M&A conversions are high-stakes, time-boxed, and politically charged. Customers don't care about your internal complexity — they care that their payments still work Monday morning. Owning that outcome requires coordinating across organizational boundaries, keeping a clear critical path, and treating client communication as a delivery dependency, not an afterthought. CB&T: done. PNC: in progress.
